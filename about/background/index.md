# Background

## Before bibliometrics

My background is in geomatics: a French grande école engineering degree, a dual MSc at Cranfield and ENSG, and a PhD in social geography at London Metropolitan University on participatory GIS and land use planning. After the PhD I held a postdoc at Copenhagen University, where I built a Google Maps platform for geolocated citizen science data collection and a Leaflet/CartoDB/D3.js platform for public-facing results. That work was as much about what spatial data can and cannot say as about the tools themselves.

The training was technical but also visual: cartographic label positioning, colour representation, and — through Tufte — the relationship between what a chart encodes and what a reader extracts from it. That instinct for visual honesty carried forward; it shows up in how I think about [bumped stacked bar charts](/projects/bumped-stacked-bar/) and in how I approach any analytical output that will be read rather than audited.

## Consultancy

From 2017 I moved into research data science at Digital Science, initially as a consultant. Seven years of building analyses for institutions, funders, and research-performing organisations that needed to understand their own position in the research landscape. I decided what a valid question looked like, what the data could and could not support, and how to make those constraints legible to people who did not want a methods lecture. The technical substrate was Google BigQuery, Python, and Looker; the actual work was deciding what to build and why.

## Thought leadership and AI analytics

In the last few years consultancy continued, but I moved into thought leadership alongside it — what one colleague described, accurately, as pre-pre-sales. I started a Substack on research data and AI and began seriously exploring AI for analytics. AI now handles the analytical layer: queries, data handling, first-pass structuring. Counterintuitively, that made the methodological design layer more explicit rather than less. When the machine handles execution, what remains is the question of what to execute, in what order, under what constraints, with what checks. I co-design workflows with AI and spend most of my time on what is reproducible, auditable, and defensible under scrutiny. I am now considerably more systematic than I was when I did everything by hand.

## What I am working on

The work I am most invested in currently is the AI metascientist: a governed architecture for conversational bibliometrics that separates interpretation from execution, embeds methodological rules in workflow infrastructure rather than prompts, and makes those rules inspectable. I also red-teamed a deployed system in the same space, which I found analytically satisfying.
